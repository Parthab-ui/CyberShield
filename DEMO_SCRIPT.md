# CYBERSHIELD — 5 to 7 Minute Viva Demonstration Script

This script is structured for a **3-member team** to present during the internal AOOP evaluation. Each member speaks for ~2 minutes, demonstrating their assigned subsystem live.

---

## ⏱ Timeline Summary
- **0:00 - 1:30** $\to$ **Member 1**: Introduction, Problem Statement, Layered Architecture, SQLite & JDBC.
- **1:30 - 3:45** $\to$ **Member 2**: Telemetry Ingestion, Threat Inheritance Hierarchy, Polymorphic Detection Engine, Live Attack Simulation.
- **3:45 - 6:00** $\to$ **Member 3**: Incident Case Management, Composition, Containment Actions (Block IP), Dashboard & Analytics.
- **6:00 - 7:00** $\to$ **Joint Q&A**: Viva questions, database persistence proof, and wrap-up.

---

## Step-by-Step Cue Sheet

### Step 1: Launch & Database Initialization (Member 1)
- **Action**: Open terminal or double-click `run.bat`.
- **Member 1 Speaks**:
  > *"Respected examiner, we present **CYBERSHIELD: Cybersecurity Threat Monitoring & Incident Response System**, built in pure Java 17 using Java Swing and SQLite.  
  > Before launching the UI, our `DatabaseManager` singleton initializes the relational database file at `data/cybershield.db`. We follow a strict **Layered Architecture**—separating `model`, `service`, `repository`, `ui`, `util`, and `exception` layers.  
  > All database access is done via native JDBC using `PreparedStatement` to prevent SQL injection, and all database operations use `try-with-resources` for automatic connection cleanup.  
  > Notice the login screen: passwords are never stored in plaintext—they are verified against SHA-256 hashes generated by our `SecurityUtils` class. Let us log in as the default analyst."*
- **Action**: Enter `analyst` and `cyber123`, then click **Login to SOC**.

---

### Step 2: Dashboard Overview & Telemetry (Member 1 & 2)
- **Visual**: The Main Dashboard opens showing the 5 top Metric Cards and initial seeded telemetry.
- **Member 1 Speaks**:
  > *"Upon login, `AuthService` establishes an in-memory session. Our custom exception hierarchy—starting from `CyberShieldException`—ensures database or authentication errors never crash the application or leak raw stack traces to the user. I now hand over to Member 2 to explain our detection engine and simulation."*
- **Member 2 Speaks**:
  > *"Thank you. Let us navigate to the **Telemetry Panel**. Here we observe raw security events. Notice how our `SecurityEvent` model uses strict **Encapsulation**: all fields are private with validated getters.  
  > When I click on an event, the lower pane displays the raw simulated network payload. Now let us demonstrate how our system detects attacks."*
- **Action**: Click on **Attack Simulator** in the sidebar.

---

### Step 3: Attack Simulator & Polymorphic Detection (Member 2)
- **Visual**: The Attack Simulator screen displays the 4 preset scenario cards and the disclaimer banner.
- **Member 2 Speaks**:
  > *"In our project, we implemented an educational simulation of a Security Operations Center.  
  > Notice our core OOP hierarchy: we created an `abstract class Threat`, which is extended by four concrete subclasses: `BruteForceThreat`, `PhishingThreat`, `MalwareThreat`, and `SuspiciousLoginThreat`.  
  > Furthermore, we have an interface called `ThreatDetector`. Our `ThreatDetectionEngine` maintains a `List<ThreatDetector>` and demonstrates **genuine Runtime Polymorphism**. When an event arrives, the engine loops through the detectors and invokes `detect()` without casting or checking concrete classes!  
  > Let us click **'SIMULATE BRUTE FORCE'**."*
- **Action**: Click the red button **SIMULATE BRUTE FORCE**.
- **Visual**: The live console displays the trace: generating 6 failed authentication events, triggering `BruteForceDetector`, detecting `THR-BF-XXXX`, and auto-escalating.
- **Member 2 Speaks**:
  > *"Look at the real-time execution console: 6 rapid failed SSH attempts were recorded. The `BruteForceDetector` counted consecutive failures within a sliding 120-second window using a Java `HashMap`. Since the threshold was exceeded, it generated a `BruteForceThreat` with `HIGH` severity.  
  > Let us now inspect the **Threat Monitor**."*
- **Action**: Click **Threat Monitor** in the sidebar and select the newly detected threat.
- **Member 2 Speaks**:
  > *"Notice the lower dossier pane: calling `threat.generateIncidentReport()` dynamically executes the overridden method of `BruteForceThreat` polymorphically! Now Member 3 will demonstrate our incident response workflow."*

---

### Step 4: Incident Escalation & Response Containment (Member 3)
- **Visual**: Switch to **Incidents** panel.
- **Member 3 Speaks**:
  > *"Thank you. In a SOC, threats are escalated into formal incident cases.  
  > This demonstrates **Composition**: an `Incident` **HAS-A** `Threat`, and an `Incident` **HAS-MANY** `ResponseAction`s stored inside a `List<ResponseAction>`.  
  > Notice the selected incident: its initial status is `OPEN`. Below, we have our containment toolbar with simulated mitigation actions: **Block IP**, **Disable User**, and **Quarantine Simulation**.  
  > Let us execute **'🛡 Block IP'** on the attacking source."*
- **Action**: Click **"🛡 Block IP"**. A confirmation dialog appears.
- **Member 3 Speaks**:
  > *"The action is recorded! Notice in the right-hand table, a new `ResponseAction` record was added to this incident's audit trail, the status transitioned to `INVESTIGATING`, and the incident was persisted to SQLite.  
  > Now, let us resolve this incident by clicking **'✓ Resolve Incident'**."*
- **Action**: Click **"✓ Resolve Incident"**, enter `"Threat contained by perimeter firewall block"`, click OK.
- **Visual**: Status updates to `RESOLVED` in green.

---

### Step 5: Analytics, Metrics & Persistence Verification (Member 3 & Member 1)
- **Action**: Click **Analytics** in the sidebar.
- **Member 3 Speaks**:
  > *"In our **Analytics Panel**, we present real-time security metrics: threat distribution by category, severity distribution, incident status breakdown, and the complete containment audit trail. Everything is built natively with Java Swing—no heavy external chart dependencies.  
  > Returning to the **Dashboard**, all 5 top metric cards have automatically updated: total events, threats, and resolved incidents reflect our live actions."*
- **Member 1 Concludes**:
  > *"Finally, to prove that our SQLite database truly persists data across application restarts, let us close the application and re-launch it."*
- **Action**: Close the window, re-launch via `run.bat`, log in.
- **Visual**: All previous telemetry events, threats, incidents, and response actions remain present in the tables!
- **Member 1**:
  > *"As you can see, all data is retained intact. This concludes our live demonstration of CYBERSHIELD. We are ready for your viva questions."*
